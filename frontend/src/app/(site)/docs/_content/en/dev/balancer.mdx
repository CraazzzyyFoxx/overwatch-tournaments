Teams can be built in two ways: hand the pool to a solver, or run a live captain draft. A tournament picks one. Beside them live mixes — workspace custom games with their own, simpler engine. This page covers what each of them optimizes, what states exist and how all of it looks from outside; the full route list is in the [endpoint reference](/api/docs).

## Team formation

`tournament.team_formation` is a string defaulting to `balancer`. Two values are used in the code:

| Value | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| `balancer` | The pool goes to the solver; an organizer accepts one variant and exports it into tournament teams |
| `draft` | Captains pick through the pool live, and the export comes from the finished draft session |

Teams that captains assembled during registration are a third, separate export path into tournament teams; they are covered in [Registration and rosters](/docs/dev/registration). Changing `team_formation` or the roster shape while a draft session is active is refused with 400, naming the status of the session in the way.

## The balancer

### Shared preparation

Before either engine runs, the pool goes through the same preparation.

- Players who cannot play any needed role are dropped. If some role of the roster shape has no capable player at all, the run errors.
- Team count is the pool size divided by the team size, floored, and then clamped by the engine's own ceiling.
- A remainder no longer blocks the run — the leftover players simply sit out. Players flagged `must_play` are never among them: they are moved to the front before the tail is cut, and only if there are more of them than slots exist does the request fail. Within the rest, `rotation_priority` ascending decides who is trimmed first.
- With captains enabled, the top-rated players are marked captains and pinned to a role: the first one from their own preferences they can play, otherwise the first available one. Captain ranking is deterministic — by descending maximum rating, ties broken by player identifier.
- Then a backtracking search finds a feasible role assignment that never evicts a captain. No assignment means an error naming which roles are short.

### The tournament engine

The tournament solver is a multi-objective evolutionary search in Rust that builds an arbitrary number of teams and optimizes **two objectives at once**:

- **balance** — the spread of total team strength, the worst gap between teams, the spread of averages, skew across role lines, the spread inside a team and a separately computed tank balance (the gap between adjacent-strength tanks and the spread of the tank line), plus a penalty for pairs of low-ranked players landing on the same roster;
- **comfort** — role discomfort: zero for a flex player on any role they can play, a rising cost for a role below their priority, a distinctly larger one for a playable but unwanted role and larger still for an unplayable one. The worst pain in the tournament, the per-team maximum pain averaged across teams and sub-role collisions inside one roster belong here too.

The result is a set of non-dominated variants for the organizer to choose from. Every run has a hard wall-clock budget (`time_limit_ms`, 600000 ms by default and at most): once spent, evolution and polishing stop and the best result found so far is returned. That is a deliberate trade of same-seed reproducibility for latency — ordinary runs converge long before the limit.

### The mix engine

The mix engine is a vendored C++ core that enumerates **every** legal split by bitmask with early pruning, and works on exactly **two equal-size teams**. It is built for Linux only; where the extension is unavailable, a mix balance silently falls back to the tournament engine.

The `mix_comfort_tilt` knob tilts quality between "even strength" and "more preferred roles"; the midpoint reproduces the engine's own weights. The candidate pruning threshold is not a requirement: if even the best split scores above it the engine errors, and the request is retried without the threshold. Since the enumeration is already finished by the time results are trimmed, a mix is handed a deep list of options rather than a top ten.

### Calling it

A tournament pool is assembled server-side: the request body carries no players at all, so a browser cannot hand the algorithm a different set of ranks than the draft sees.

```http
POST /api/v1/balancer/tournaments/42/balance HTTP/1.1
Host: owt.craazzzyyfoxx.me
Authorization: Bearer <session token or API key>
Content-Type: application/json

{}
```

The answer is 202 with a `job_id` plus `status_url` / `result_url`; status reads `queued`, `running`, `succeeded` or `failed`. There is also a synchronous twin for integrations — `POST /api/v1/balancer/balance?workspace_id=1`, where the whole pool travels in `player_data`, the roster shape in `role_mask`, and the teams come back in the same response. It reads nothing from the workspace, its solver budget is clamped to 60 seconds, and a pool that will not settle in time answers 504 and belongs on the asynchronous job API.

## The live draft

### The session

Session states: `setup → ready → live ⇄ paused → completed | cancelled`.

Formats are `snake`, `linear` and `custom`. Under `custom`, each round carries its own rule: `linear`, `reverse`, `weakest_first`, `strongest_first`, `team_avg_asc`, `team_avg_desc`. Captain order is configured separately: `manual` (selection order), `weakest_first`, `strongest_first` or `random` — a deterministic Mulberry32 shuffle seeded from the settings, so a preview and its commit always agree. The autopick strategy is `best_fit` (default), `best_available` or `role_need`.

The round count is never submitted: it is derived from the roster shape as `team_size - 1`. A shape that leaves no rounds is refused.

### Clock and concurrency

The clock is server-authoritative. A supervisor runs it, ownership of a session's clock is held by a distributed lock with a 10-second TTL renewed every 3 seconds, and the client only renders the deadline it is sent.

A manual pick and an autopick are the same conditional `UPDATE`, guarded by both the on-clock state and the optimistic `version` token. Exactly one writer updates the row; the loser gets **409 `pick_already_resolved`**.

```http
POST /api/v1/balancer/draft/picks/981/select HTTP/1.1
Host: owt.craazzzyyfoxx.me
Authorization: Bearer <session token>
Content-Type: application/json

{ "player_id": 314, "expected_version": 17, "target_role": "damage" }
```

Autopick takes the same `expected_version`. Separately, `draft_session_stale` (409) guards session setup: once the seed has been rebuilt, a stale preview cannot be committed.

### Autopick and pauses

Autopick only chooses from player-role pairs proven **safe**. Safety is a bipartite matching between the remaining slots and the available players, and a hypothetical pick is tested **before** it is applied, so it cannot starve another team's slot. When no safe choice exists the draft does **not** force a pick: it pauses with reason `role_shortage`.

The second pause reason is `order_recalculated`. The `team_avg_asc` and `team_avg_desc` rules re-seat their round by live averages when it starts, so the order can move on that round's first pick and the draft stops so everyone sees it.

Candidates come from the same roster engine the balance run reads: a player is scored at the rank of the role they would actually fill, and a player the balancer can rank on no role is not a candidate at all rather than a rank-zero last resort. The fit formula is deliberately simpler than the balancer's — autopick and suggestions have to answer synchronously and deterministically.

## Mixes and custom games

Custom game statuses are `draft`, `balanced`, `completed`, `cancelled`. A player's participation in the pool is `must_play`, `pool` or `benched`; roles are taken either from every ranked role (`all_ranked`) or from an explicit list (`explicit`).

Caps: at most 8 teams and at most 16 co-hosts per game. Permissions come from a dedicated workspace system role, **host**: everything an ordinary member has plus full authorship over the `custom_game` resource — workspace membership alone is not enough to run a mix. Only the host writes the pool; some actions are open to co-hosts as well.

The rotation recommendation for the next map ranks the pool on one key, checked in order: longest current sit-out streak, then shortest current played-in-a-row streak, then fewest maps played this game, then input order for determinism. Pinned `must_play` members always keep a seat and rank ahead of everyone. If the seats cover the whole pool, or none of the unpinned members has any history yet, everyone comes back `neutral` — sort stability must not be dressed up as a fairness verdict.

## Realtime

Draft state and balancer state travel on different topics.

| Topic | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| `tournament:{id}:draft` | Draft events: `draft.pick_started`, `draft.paused`, `draft.resumed`, `draft.blocked`, `draft.completed`, `draft.cancelled`, `draft.session_updated`, `draft.player_updated` |
| `tournament:{id}:balancer` | Data edits (`balancer.registrations_changed`, `balancer.balance_saved`, `balancer.teams_changed`, `balancer.config_changed`) and job lifecycle (`balancer_job.queued` / `running` / `progress` / `succeeded` / `failed`) |
| `draft:{session_id}:chat` | The draft room chat — its own topic with its own permissions; re-seeding a session creates a new room |
| `workspace:{id}:pickup_mix` | A workspace's mix events |

Job progress ticks are not persisted — a late joiner takes a snapshot from the job status read instead. Also ephemeral are `balancer.presence`, synthesized by the gateway from live connections, and `balancer.drag`, the only event type the gateway accepts from a client.

A team export announces staleness separately, as an invalidation of tournament resources: draft events themselves invalidate nothing, because a pick writes only to draft tables.
