A result in OWT lives on three levels, and confusing them is expensive: an encounter is the series, an encounter game is one position of it and the only carrier of result authority, and a match is the parsed log of one map and the carrier of statistics. This article covers all three, plus captain reports, pick-ban, scrims and log ingestion.

## The three levels

| Object | What it is | What is authoritative on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Encounter (`encounter`) | a best-of series between two teams | the final series score and bracket advancement |
| Encounter game (`encounter_game`) | one position of the series: its map and its result | the accepted map score and `result_source` |
| Match (`matches.match`) | the parsed log of one played map | statistics, kill feed, events |

The key point: **a match does not decide the result**. It records what a log *observed*; an encounter game records what the tournament *decided*. Captain reports no longer write `matches.match` rows — a match row comes from a parsed log. A match's `source` may be `log_parser` or `captain_report`, but only `log_parser` is written today; `captain_report` survives on historical rows. An encounter's `has_logs` flag is computed as "a match with `source = log_parser` exists", so captain confirmations alone never light it up.

## Encounter games

A game carries `position` (1-based), `map_id`, `state`, the accepted score and its provenance:

- `state`: `planned` (the position exists, no map chosen yet), `awaiting_result` (map known, waiting for both sides' claims), `disputed` (the claims disagree), `confirmed` (a score was accepted), `cancelled` (kept as history after an undo or reset; never revived).
- `teams`: one line per participation seated in the game (`encounter_team_id`, `score`, `placement`, `column_values`, `starting_side`), `result_source` (`captain_agreement`, `admin`, `admin_log`), `confirmed_at`.
- `result_version` is bumped on every accepted-result write (confirmation, correction) and never decreases — clients and events use it to tell a fresh result from a stale one.

The database enforces two invariants: among non-cancelled games there is at most one game per position of an encounter, and the `confirmed` state requires the full shape — both accepted scores, `result_source` and `confirmed_at`.

## Map reports

Each side files its own claim against a specific **game**, never against a map: a series may play the same map twice, and only the position tells the two plays apart.

```http
POST /api/v1/encounters/512/games/1841/report HTTP/1.1
Host: owt.craazzzyyfoxx.me
Authorization: Bearer <token>
Content-Type: application/json

{ "basis_version": 1, "results": [{ "encounter_team_id": 71, "score": 2 }, { "encounter_team_id": 72, "score": 1 }] }
```

There is at most one claim per game per participation. Every `results` line names a participation (`encounter_team_id`), never a side or the reporter, and `basis_version` is the game's `result_version` the claim was made against — a correction landing in between answers 409 `stale_state` instead of silently winning. Then:

- **The claims match** — the game becomes `confirmed` with `result_source = captain_agreement`, the series score is recomputed, and the next map opens (for progressive pick-ban rounds this is the "that map just finished" signal).
- **The claims disagree** — the game becomes `disputed`, both claims stand, and the organizer is notified.
- **The game is already `confirmed`** — the claim is rejected with the code `result_locked`.
- **The stage bracket is a preview** — the write is rejected with 409: captains do not report against an unpublished bracket.

Only an admin can change a confirmed result: `POST /api/v1/admin/encounters/{encounter_id}/games/{game_id}/result`, and the correction must carry a reason, which goes into the journal. The correction is dependency-aware, in every game: when a pick-ban room's first-ban rotation follows results (`result_*`), position N's outcome is what decided who opens round N+1, so a correction that changes that opener makes the following round wrong. While nobody has used that round, it is rebuilt for the corrected opener in the same commit. Once it is in use (a submission on one of its steps, or its game already has a side, a claim or a confirmed result), the correction is refused with 409 `dependent_round_started` before anything is written, the audit row included, rather than deleting real history: the captains undo that round, or the organizer resets the room, before the correction is retried; a game already claimed or confirmed is recovered by reopening the result and resetting the rooms, which replaces the encounter's games. A correction that leaves the opener alone (2:1 to 3:1) changes no round at all.

The result journal is read from `GET /api/v1/admin/encounters/{encounter_id}/result-audit`. Its actions are `confirm`, `reopen`, `auto_confirm`, `auto_dispute`, `auto_pending`, `import`, `cascade_reset`, plus the three per-game ones: `game_confirm`, `game_correct`, `game_cancel`.

## The series score

The series score is the number of confirmed, non-cancelled games each participation won. While the encounter is live, it is materialised onto the participations' `teams[].score` on every accepted result. Once the encounter is `completed`, the official finalize is the sole writer of that score — the single path taken by captain auto-confirmation, an admin decision and a Challonge import alike. Finalize is also what triggers bracket advancement (see [Tournaments, stages and brackets](/docs/dev/tournaments)).

## The series report

Besides per-map claims there is a report on the whole series, one per encounter and team. It carries `basis_version`, `games_played` (draws included) and one `results` line per live participation (`encounter_team_id`, `score`, optional `placement` and `wins`), an optional `closeness` rating from 1 to 10, a comment, the organizer's custom text fields, and per-map replay codes.

Two reports with the same score auto-confirm the encounter: `result_status` becomes `confirmed`, the encounter's `closeness` becomes the average of the two ratings divided by 10 (so 0…1), and advancement runs. Differing scores produce `disputed`. While only one report exists, `result_status` is `pending_confirmation`. After `confirmed`, only an admin can change the result (`POST /api/v1/admin/encounters/{encounter_id}/result`, and `.../result/reopen` to un-confirm it).

The form is configured per tournament: the built-in fields `closeness`, `map_codes` and `comment` are individually enabled and made required, plus custom text fields. Disabled fields are **dropped** from a submitted body rather than rejected with 422.

- `GET /api/v1/encounters/{encounter_id}/my-role` — which side the current user is on.
- `POST /api/v1/encounters/{encounter_id}/report` — submit a series report.
- `GET /api/v1/encounters/{encounter_id}/reports` — read the reports.

## Pick-ban

One engine serves two kinds: `map` and `hero` (`kind` travels as a path segment). Configuration is resolved through a **tournament → stage → round** cascade, separately per kind: a map config and a hero config can sit side by side at the same level. A level with an empty pool is a rules template, not a room — no session opens from it.

Modes are `pool` (one shared pool) and `slots` (one slot per map of the series, each with at least two candidates and an optional reserve map for a draw). What is played is a **ruleset** (`ruleset_json`): phases picked by a `when` condition on the round, each holding steps. A step names its `action` (ban/pick/protect/decider), its `actors` (`first`, `second`, `both`, `home`, `away`, `winner_prev`, `loser_prev`, `system`), how many items each acting side takes (`count`/`min`), whether it is `blind`, whether every item names an opponent player (`target`), how many maps a ban lives (`lifetime`), its timer and timeout policy, its dispute allowance, and condition trees for what may be chosen (`eligible`) plus `constraints`. A phase may instead carry a `generator` (`bracket`, `slot_veto`), which builds the map-veto steps from `best_of` and the pool size.

A session only opens when everything lines up at once: both teams are known, the stage is published, a config is found and its pool is non-empty, slot mode has enough slots for `best_of` and each of them is filled, both sides pressed ready (`POST /api/v1/encounters/{encounter_id}/ready` — one confirmation covers both kinds), and for hero round N map N is already settled. When there is no session, the state reports why: `teams_unknown`, `bracket_preview`, `not_configured`, `slot_count_mismatch`, `slot_underfilled`, `not_ready`, `waiting_map`. Opening the session is a WRITE, performed by the room healer right after the write that lifted the last gate (and by a one-minute backstop sweep), never by the state read — which is pure: no lock, no write, no commit. The healer is also what settles an expired step (a one-second tick), appends a hero round, repairs the series' games and opens the next freeplay position, and it signals the room on `encounter:{id}:map-veto` / `encounter:{id}:pick-ban:hero` when it lands.

Rounds can be progressive: slot mode and every hero config grow one round per map, so round 2 cannot start before map 1 has been played. A flat map config is the classic veto that settles the whole series' map order up front.

Further rules:

- Cross-round memory is a condition, not a column: a phase's `pool_filter` and a step's `eligible` read leaves such as `banned_by`/`picked_by` (`self`/`opponent`/`any` over `series`/`round`/`previous_round`), `item_group`, `item_in` and `target_role_match`. Constraints (`one_per_target`, `max_per_group`, `min_per_group`) are checked on every submission.
- A step's items are a **submission**, not a row per action: an open step appends one item at a time and publishes it immediately, a blind step keeps each side's draft private until every side locked, and duplicate bans from both sides merge into one banned entry that both submissions still list. A ban with a `lifetime` past this map is re-created on the next round as a carried entry.
- The first pick is decided by the `higher_seed` rule. First-ban rotation is `fixed`, `alternate`, `result_winner_first`, `result_loser_first` or `result_loser_choice`. The last one parks the session on `awaiting_choice`: only the side named in `pending_loser_side` may pick the opener, through `elect-opener`.
- Undoing the last step is a two-sided consent: one side asks (`undo_requested_by` and `undo_target_index` are recorded), and the opponent's matching call applies it. Any new action clears the request, and an undo is refused once the round has been played or a later round has opened. A revealed blind step can also be redone unilaterally through `dispute`, up to the step's `dispute.max`. Admins have their own `pick-ban-act`, `pick-ban-submit`, `pick-ban-reopen`, `pick-ban-elect-opener` and session reset.
- Readiness itself is overridable: `POST /api/v1/admin/encounters/{encounter_id}/readiness` with `{side, ready}` presses (or un-presses) a side's ready for an unreachable captain and answers `{readiness}`. Clearing is a 409 once a session of either kind exists — readiness gates session creation only, so resetting the session is the way back. A captain's own ready now signals `encounter:{id}:map-veto` too: nothing polls a room while no session exists, so without it the opposite captain would wait for a reload.
- A room that goes wrong has four organizer controls, all `POST /api/v1/admin/encounters/{encounter_id}/…` under `match.result` and all journaled with the reason given: `pick-ban-pause` `{kind, paused}` freezes a kind — the deadline disappears and nothing times out (system steps and reveals still settle, so an organizer acting during the pause sees the board move), and every captain write answers 409 `Pick-ban session is paused` while the admin overrides keep working; resuming returns exactly the time the step had left, and a step that opened during the pause gets its full timer. `pick-ban-extend` `{kind, seconds}` (10..3600) moves the open step's start forward and works while paused; 409 without a timed step. `pick-ban-cancel` `{kind, reason}` retires a session: a cancelled hero session stops opening rounds, a cancelled map session hands the series back to freeplay — settled maps keep their positions, later ones are named by the captains — and the way back from either is the session reset.
- `technical-loss` `{loser_team_id, results?, reason}` ends a match nobody is going to play: every live session is cancelled, every position that is not confirmed is cancelled, and the result is confirmed in the same transaction (the same guards as `/result`, so 409 on a preview bracket, on a stage already seeded from this result, and on an already confirmed one). Omit the score and it is derived — the winner takes `best_of // 2 + 1` wins or more if it already had them, the forfeiting participation keeps the maps it really won capped one short of winning, so a Bo5 standing 2:0 for the participation that forfeits is recorded 2:3.
- `GET /api/v1/admin/tournaments/{tournament_id}/pregame-rooms` is the organizer's board of every room of a tournament: one row per encounter with readiness, each kind's open step and deadline, the series' game counts, a single `phase` (`teams_unknown`, `readiness`, `map`, `hero`, `report`, `done`, `idle`) and `attention` flags (`game_disputed`, `result_disputed`, `awaiting_choice`, `overdue`, `late_not_ready`). Strictly read-only, like the room's own state read: neither creates a session nor opens a position.
- `GET /api/v1/admin/encounters/{encounter_id}/room-history` is that room's journal: everything the room wrote down — readiness, session opened/reset/completed, each round, every captain and organizer action, reveals, timeouts, disputes, undo, map and series reports, and the four emergency controls — merged with the encounter's result audit and returned newest first (`limit` 1..500, default 200). Each entry names its `origin` (`room`/`result`), `source` (`captain`/`admin`/`system`), `side`, actor and `reason`, with an action-specific `data` object. The journal is keyed by the encounter, not the session, so resetting a session scraps the draft and keeps the story.

The room is read and driven through `GET /api/v1/encounters/{encounter_id}/pick-ban/{kind}/state` and `POST .../act`, `.../submit`, `.../dispute`, `.../elect-opener`, `.../undo`; the leaf/constraint catalog through `GET /api/v1/pick-ban-rules/catalog`, and a tournament's configs through `GET /api/v1/tournaments/{tournament_id}/pick-ban-configs`. Live updates go to `encounter:{id}:map-veto` (maps) and `encounter:{id}:pick-ban:hero` (heroes) — two topics rather than one, because their audiences and permissions differ; the room's chat is `encounter:{id}:chat`.

## Scrims

A scrim is the same pipeline cut down to a single encounter. A room is one stage, two rosterless teams and one encounter, published immediately: the stage is created with `is_published = true`, so a room has no preview state.

The creator takes slot 1; slot 2 stays unclaimed and is claimed with the token from the link — an address, not an access secret: any workspace member can read the room. The viewer's slot (`viewer_slot`) is resolved server-side from the team's captain, never from a client parameter. One person cannot captain both teams. The number of open rooms per user and the maximum `best_of` are capped by a platform setting; closing one frees the slot while the room itself stays readable.

```http
POST /api/v1/scrims/{token}/claim HTTP/1.1
Host: owt.craazzzyyfoxx.me
Authorization: Bearer <token>
```

The routes are `POST /api/v1/scrims`, `GET /api/v1/scrims`, `GET /api/v1/scrims/{token}`, `POST /api/v1/scrims/{token}/claim` and `POST /api/v1/scrims/{token}/close`. Every room of a workspace lives in one hidden container tournament, and standings recalculation is skipped on it: by construction the container has no bracket, no standings and no rosters.

`GET /api/v1/scrims` takes an optional `scope`: `mine` (default) lists the caller's own rooms, `workspace` lists every room of the workspace and requires `match.result` on it. That same grant lets staff close a room they neither created nor play; each serialized room carries `can_close`, which is the single rule the UI renders the action from.

## Match logs

**Ingestion.** Three paths: an organizer's multipart upload (`POST /api/v1/admin/logs/upload` with `tournament_id`, an optional `encounter_id` and one or more `files[]`), the Discord bot's queue-borne ingest, and a manual processing request (`POST /api/v1/logs/{id}`). The source is recorded on the record as `upload`, `discord` or `manual`. The filename is checked for path traversal, the content must be valid UTF-8, and the file lands in object storage before the database row is written. An upload whose log has no `match_end` row is refused per file before it is stored — the match is unfinished or the game is still writing it — which is what lets the browser's automatic upload retry it later.

**Deduplication.** A SHA-256 is taken over the file's bytes. If a record in state `done` with the same hash already exists for the same tournament and filename, the file is not reprocessed. An existing `pending` or `failed` record for the same filename is reused, with the attempt counter reset to zero: the content may have changed, so the retry budget starts over.

**Mix logs.** The same upload accepts a mix match: instead of `tournament_id` it takes `custom_game_id` and exactly one of `encounter_id` (a match of that mix) or `lobby_index` (zero-based; the server takes the lobby's live match, else its latest recorded one, and answers 422 `no_mix_match` when there is neither). The mix's host, a co-host or a superuser may upload, or anyone holding `log.create` in its workspace, closed mixes included. The record gets `hint_tournament_id = null` and the object is stored under `logs/mix/{custom_game_id}/{hash}`. A log never decides a mix match's result — the host records it — and never adds players to a lineup. A mix match's statistics are visible only inside the mix: `GET /api/v1/matches/{id}` serves them without the tournament visibility check, while profiles, comparisons, records, achievements and analytics do not count them. Cancelling or undoing the match deletes its statistics; uploading the same file to the mix again re-parses the record left without a match. A mix history row (`rpc.balancer.custom.match_history`) carries `stats_match_id` and `log_state` (`processing` / `failed`), and `/api/v1/admin/logs/history` carries `custom_game_id` and `custom_game_name`.

**Mix match import.** A game with a match fetcher (`capabilities.match_fetch`) also takes a mix match by its id in the game's own records: `POST /api/v1/custom-games/{custom_game_id}/matches/{match_id}/match-import` with `{external_id}` (`match_id` is the mix match's encounter), and `GET` on the same path lists the requests, newest first, with the organizer warnings. Same audience as the mix upload, closed mixes included; 404 for another mix's match. The request is a `stats.match_fetch` row with `hint_tournament_id = null`, retried for 24 h like a tournament game's; the fetched bytes are stored under `logs/mix/{custom_game_id}/{hash}` and ingested like a mix log — statistics only. `participants_dropped` counts the match's seats the observation does not show. While the newest import waits, the history row's `log_state` reads `processing`, and `failed` once it gives up. After an undo, filing the same id for the re-recorded match re-points the match's job left without a match.

**Record states** are `pending`, `processing`, `done`, `failed`; a record also carries `error_message`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `attempts`, `content_hash` and an optional encounter attachment. The queue and history are read by admins at `/api/v1/admin/logs/queue-status`, `/api/v1/admin/logs/history` and `/api/v1/admin/logs/stats`, with `POST /api/v1/admin/logs/{id}/retry` to retry; its optional `encounter_id` body field attaches an unfinished log to that encounter first. Without an attachment, a team pair that meets more than once is told apart by the log's map — the round map pool that admits it, and no series that already holds that map from another log — and anything still ambiguous fails with `encounter_ambiguous`. Progress reaches subscribers as the `workspace.logs` resource going stale.

**What parsing produces.** One `matches.match` row per map with `source = log_parser`, a duration, the log filename and a link to the processing record; player statistics as name-and-value rows, per round and again as a whole-match total (round `0`), broken down by hero where the metric is hero-specific; a kill feed with time, round, fight number, killer, victim, heroes, damage and the critical-hit and environmental flags; and events — assists, ultimates, hero swaps, resurrections. These are read from `GET /api/v1/matches`, `GET /api/v1/matches/{id}` and `GET /api/v1/matches/{id}/kill-feed`. A match lists every participant in seat order (`participants`: seat, source position, optional source team, the official result line of its bound game and the team facts the log observed), not a home/away pair; `GET /api/v1/matches/{id}` adds the members, segments and facts of every grain, and the kill-feed route returns the whole event timeline of the match's own game.

**Impact and MVP.** A player's contribution is scored on top of the parsed match, per round and for the whole map. The kill feed is cut into fights: a new fight starts on a round change or after a lull longer than fifteen seconds between kills, and fight boundaries give first picks and first deaths. Each hero a player was on is a separate stint with a class (tank, damage, support); its statistics are turned into rates per ten minutes and compared against baselines — one per hero when the hero has enough played maps, otherwise one per role — so picking a hero with an unusual stat line earns nothing. Outliers are clipped, the stat deviations are combined with per-role weights fitted to map wins, and each role's composite is standardised, so the chance of an MVP per seat is the same for every role. A short appearance is shrunk towards zero rather than cut, and the result is scaled by the player's share of the longest played time; a player under a minute on classed heroes is not scored at all. The result is stored as `impact_points`, with `impact_rank` as the competition rank (1 is best, ties share a place; absent for an unscored player, never `0`). `overperformance_score` measures the same score against what is expected of a player of the role actually played at their rank quintile inside the same tournament or mix; the top overperformance of a map that clears the frozen 95th-percentile threshold also stores `overperformance_badge`. For the whole map the per-stat shares of the score are stored as `contrib_*` facts, which the MVP tooltip shows. Baselines, role scales, expectations and the badge threshold are built from the last twelve months and frozen per formula version (`impact_v2`); changing them means a version bump and a full backfill. `impact_rank` is what ratings, analytics and MVP achievements read.

## Who consumes this

**Achievements.** An achievement rule is a declarative JSON condition tree evaluated against match data, standings and bracket path; a player's effective set is the evaluation plus manual grants minus manual revokes. Recalculation runs by itself after a log parses successfully, or manually (`POST /api/v1/achievement/calculate`, `POST /api/v1/achievement/calculate/{tournament_id}`); public reads are `GET /api/v1/achievements/user/{user_id}` and `GET /api/v1/achievements/{id}/users`.

**Analytics.** Ratings and forecasts are built from the same encounters and matches: an analytics run replays encounters in chronological order, and match quality (competitiveness, predictability, skill balance) is scored separately from series scores and pre-encounter team ratings. Reads are `/api/v1/analytics`, `/api/v1/analytics/match-quality`, `/api/v1/analytics/performance` and neighbouring routes; a workspace may have only one active computation job at a time. The tables behind all of this are described in [Data model](/docs/dev/data-model) and drawn on the [Database schema](/docs/dev/schema) page.

<Callout type="warning">
Correcting a game's result is destructive to whatever was built on the old outcome: unplayed pick-ban rounds are rebuilt, and if they have already been touched the correction is refused outright. Do not design an integration that assumes an already-opened round is stable.
</Callout>
