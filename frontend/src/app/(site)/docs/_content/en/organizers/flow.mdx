A tournament is a graph of steps. Registrations, team formations and stages are boxes; arrows between them say who moves on to the next step. The graph lives in the **Flow** sub-tab of **Bracket** (**Bracket → Flow**, after **Stages** and, for FFA, **Lobbies**). It is the advanced tool, and most tournaments never need it: every tournament starts with a **Registration** and a **Formation** box already connected, and you build ordinary shapes in **Bracket** — **Add stage** wires a new stage to the previous one, and the **Who advances** block of a stage sets who goes through (see [Stages and brackets](/docs/organizers/brackets)). The team formation method is set in the **General** card of a stage the formation feeds (row **Teams formed by**), or on the formation box here. Open **Flow** for what **Bracket** cannot express: a re-formation before the playoff, an individual Swiss with a draft, a league season that runs over several days, or a custom filter such as a range or a manual pick.

<Callout type="warning">
Everything that changes a node needs the workspace permission to update tournaments: saving the graph, a node's settings and schedule, and moving a node between statuses. A role that may only create tournaments or teams can see **Bracket → Flow** only if it can also read tournaments, and cannot change anything on it. See [Roles and permissions](/docs/organizers/roles).
</Callout>

## Nodes

There are eight kinds of node. The first three are steps with a lifecycle of their own; the other five compute a list from other lists and need no one to start them.

| Node | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Registration** | One intake of players: a form, a sign-up window, optional check-in. It hands the approved (and checked-in) players on. Its statuses are **Draft**, **Open**, **Check-in**, **Closed**. |
| **Formation** | Turns players into teams in one of four ways: **Balancer**, **Draft**, **Registered teams** or **Manual**. Its statuses are **Draft**, **Forming**, **Completed**. |
| **Stage** | A group stage, bracket, Swiss or FFA league. It takes teams (players, for an individual Swiss), plays them out and hands on its ranking. Its statuses are **Draft**, **Ready**, **Live**, **Completed**. |
| **Filter** | Keeps part of a ranking: **Top N**, a **Range**, **Top N per group**, a **Swiss status** (qualified or eliminated) or a **Manual pick**. This is where "who goes through" is decided. |
| **Merge** | Joins several lists of the same kind into one, in the order the arrows were drawn; a repeated participant is kept once. |
| **Split** | Cuts a ranking into buckets of a given size, in order or in a snake order, each leaving through its own output. |
| **Aggregate** | Adds up the results of several stages into one score per participant. See the League season section below. |
| **Leaderboard** | The final table of the tournament. It is what the public **Table** tab shows, and it takes the places you pin. |

The pure nodes show **Pending** (an input has no results yet), **Tentative** (results are in, but some input is still playing) or **Final** (every input is final).

## Building

Open **Admin → Tournaments →** your tournament **→ Bracket → Flow**.

1. Add a node from the **Nodes** palette: drag it onto the canvas, or click it. The palette has two groups, **Lifecycle** and **Transitions**. On a narrow screen the palette is the **Add node** menu at the top left of the canvas.
2. Draw an arrow by dragging from a dot on the right side of one node to a dot on the left side of another. Dots have a colour: blue carries players, amber carries teams. You can connect dots of the same colour; the one exception is a team list into a **Formation**, which dissolves the teams back into players.
3. Click a node to open its settings in the side panel.
4. Watch the **Issues** panel in the top right corner of the canvas: it re-checks the graph while you work. An error blocks saving (an arrow loop, mismatched colours, two arrows into a one-input dot, an aggregate that needs more inputs than it has); a warning only points something out. The panel stays folded to its counts until an error appears. Click a row to select its node and bring it into view.
5. Press **Save flow** in the bar at the bottom of the page, which sums up what you changed. The whole change is saved at once, or not at all; a save that fails says why in the same bar. **Discard** drops the unsaved changes.

**Arrange nodes**, the last button under the zoom controls, lays the graph out in columns from left to right. A flow whose boxes overlap opens already arranged; that layout is saved with your next change.

If your save would delete a registration, formation or stage box that was already saved, you are asked to confirm first: the dialog lists what goes with each box (entries, balancer config and draft sessions, stage groups and matches). A box that still owns teams, entries or sheet feeds cannot be deleted until you remove those. If someone else changes a box on the server while you are editing, your edit is kept, the box shows **Changed on server** and the **Issues** list says so; boxes you did not touch simply take the new server settings.

Everything in the side panel is part of the graph: a change there is saved with **Save flow**, together with the arrows.

A node you added appears with the status **Draft**. A stage needs its groups or brackets: add them in the stage's settings under **Items** before you start it.

## Presets

On a tournament that still has only its starting boxes, **Choose a preset** at the top of the canvas offers a ready-made graph. The preset reuses the starting **Registration** and **Formation**, so nothing is duplicated.

| Preset | What it builds |
|---|---|
| **Single stage** | Formation → one **Playoff** stage. |
| **Groups → playoff** | Formation → **Groups** (round robin) → filter **Top 2 per group** → **Playoff** (single elimination, seeded across groups). |
| **Groups → re-formation → playoff** | Formation → **Groups** → filter **Top 4** → a second formation, **Playoff teams**, → **Playoff**. The second formation starts in the same mode as the first. |
| **Individual Swiss → draft → playoff** | Registration → **Swiss** (individual, 4 playoff teams) → filter **Qualifiers** (top 20) → the formation, now in **Draft** mode → **Playoff**. 20 qualifiers make 4 teams of 5. |
| **League season (N days, best K)** | For each of N days (2 to 10): **Day i** registration → **Day i teams** formation → **Day i** round-robin stage. All days feed an **Aggregate** named **Season**, which feeds a **Leaderboard** named **Season table**. |

Change the numbers after you apply a preset if they do not fit: they are plain filter and stage settings.

A preset adds stages, not their contents. Open each stage's settings and add the groups or bracket under **Items** before you move the stage to **Ready**.

## Running nodes

Each node is moved on its own screen: a registration and a formation from the status controls at the top of the tournament page, a stage from its actions in **Bracket → Stages**. A registration opens, closes and runs check-in on its own schedule; a formation goes **Forming**, then **Completed** when you export the teams; a stage goes **Ready** (inputs are seated), **Live** (matches are generated), then **Completed** by itself when every match is finished. Several stages may be live at the same time, which is how the days of a league run.

The side panel keeps only what shapes the graph, plus one button to the node's own screen:

- **Registration**: **Who registers** and **Require check-in**; **All registration settings** opens the rest (approval, late sign-ups, capacity) in **Settings → Registration**. The sign-up schedule is in **Settings → Schedule**, the entries on the **Registration** tab.
- **Formation**: the mode (and the draft format in **Draft** mode), changeable while the node is **Draft**, with a button to the matching screen: **Open the draft**, **Open the balancer**, **Open registered teams** or **Open the roster**.
- **Stage**: its format, and **Open in the stage editor** for everything else (items, seeding, best-of, standings).
- **Filter, Merge, Split, Aggregate, Leaderboard**: their settings; the leaderboard also shows its table, where you pin places.

What freezes:

- A node that has started (it left **Draft**) can no longer be deleted or re-configured, and neither can the arrows leading into it.
- A filter, aggregate or other computing node freezes once any step after it has started.
- You can always add a new step at the end: a new day, a new group stage. A running league appends its next day this way.

A formation that is **Completed** can go back to **Forming** only while nothing after it has started.

**Reopen** is offered on every closed registration and completed formation. If a later step has already started, the platform refuses and says so.

## League season

A league season is one tournament whose days are stages. Build it with the **League season (N days, best K)** preset: **Days** is N, **Best results counted** is K.

The **Aggregate** node adds up what each participant scored on each day. With K smaller than N the mode is **Best N**: the K best days count, the rest are ignored. With K equal to N every day counts. The score of a day is set on the aggregate:

- **Stage points**: the points the participant had in that day's standings;
- **Points by place**: a table of place → points, 0 for a place that is not in it;
- **Formula**: an expression over `place`, `participants`, `points`, `wins`, `draws`, `losses`, `map_diff`, `buchholz` and `ffa_score`.

A day the participant did not play counts as 0 in **Sum of all** and **Last N**, is skipped in **Best N**, and is left out of the divisor in **Average**. Participants are ordered by the total; a tie goes to the one with more best places (most firsts, then most seconds), then to the better place on the latest day. A tie that remains can be settled with a pin on the **Leaderboard**.

**Ranking by role** (players only) ranks every role on its own: Tank, Damage and Support become separate rankings with their own places and their own best K days. A player who tanked on some days and supported on others gets a row in each, and the days of one role never add to the other. The role of a day is the player's role in that day's team. A ranking by role can only feed a **Leaderboard**; a pin there places the player inside the chosen role.

The **Table** tab shows the result live while the season runs, and the **Aggregate** node shows the same numbers as a preview.

## What players see

- **Table** — the **Leaderboard**: one column per day, the results that count highlighted and marked, and above it the rule in words ("Only the best 2 results count."). Search by player name, sort by any column (places stay the official ones), and hover or tap a day for its team, role, wins–draws–losses and Buchholz. With **Ranking by role** the table has one tab per role. The tab exists only for tournaments with a leaderboard node.
- **Format** — the graph itself, read-only: each box is a step with its status, arrows show who moves on.
- **Bracket** — when the tournament has several branches (for example several league days), the stages are grouped by the registrations upstream of them. With one branch, nothing changes.
- **Registration cards** — a tournament with two or more registration nodes shows one card per open intake, for example **Register for Day 3**. With one intake the usual register button stays.

<Callout>
Group tables and FFA lobby tables draw the **advances** cut line from the filter after the stage: **Top N per group** cuts every group (every lobby) at its own N (and at **upper** when it splits the passed teams between the brackets), **Top N** cuts the table only when the stage is a single group. Every such filter draws its own line. A **Range**, a **Swiss status** or a **Manual pick** draws none.
</Callout>
