A stage is one part of a tournament: a group phase, a Swiss round, or a playoff bracket. This page covers building stages, generating the bracket, and setting seeding, points and tiebreakers.

It all lives in the admin: open **Tournaments**, pick the tournament and go to the **Bracket** tab. The stage list is on the left, each stage with its phase number and how many of its matches are played; the editor for the selected stage is on the right, with the tabs **Settings**, **Bracket**, **Standings** and **Items**. **Settings** is one page of cards: **General**, **Seeding**, **Qualification & elimination** (Swiss only), **Points**, **Tiebreakers**, **Best-of** (not for elimination stages, which set it on the **Bracket** tab) and **Round schedule**. One **Save changes** at the bottom saves all of it, the round times included; a card marked **Applies immediately** saves on its own. The stage's **Bracket** tab shows its bracket — the tree as it stands or as it would be generated — and, for elimination stages, lets you redraw its structure.

## Stage formats

**Add stage** creates a stage: **Stage name**, **Stage type**, and a few extra fields for some formats. When the tournament already has stages, it also asks **Who advances from “‹last stage›”** and wires the new stage to it: the default is **Top N from each group** (2) when that stage has several groups, otherwise **Everyone**. The first stage is wired to the team formation automatically. Next to **Stages** the **Bracket** tab has **Lobbies** (FFA only) and **Flow**, the graph editor for shapes this page does not cover — see [The Flow tab](/docs/organizers/flow).

| Format | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Round Robin** | Every team in the group plays every other. Nothing advances through a bracket — the Standings are the result. With an odd number of teams, one team sits out each round. |
| **Swiss** | Each round pairs teams on similar results. **Swiss max rounds** sets how many rounds are played. |
| **Single Elimination** | Lose once and you are out. When the field is not a power of two, some teams skip the first round. |
| **Double Elimination** | An upper and a lower bracket; a team is out after its second loss. |

Swiss avoids repeating a pairing that has already been played, and it does not give a second bye to a team that already had one. If the field cannot be paired at all, generation stops with an error — add or remove a team.

<Callout>
Once a stage exists, only platform administrators can change its format: **Only superusers can modify stage type after creation.**
</Callout>

## The life of a stage

The stage header shows its status.

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Being built. A bracket may already be generated, but the stage is not ready. |
| **Ready** | The inputs are seated from the stage's input. Organizers see the bracket; captains do not. |
| **Live** | The stage is live: matches can be played and reported. Several stages can be live at once. |
| **Completed** | The stage is finished. |

**Generate bracket** builds the bracket without starting the stage — that is what preview is for. Until the stage is started, captains can neither run the map veto nor file a report: their room shows **Bracket preview** and a note saying it opens once you start the stage.

The usual order is:

1. Create the stage and wire teams into it (the **Items** tab).
2. Press **Generate bracket** and check the result in the stage's **Bracket** tab.
3. When it looks right, choose **Mark ready** (it seats the teams a playoff takes from the earlier stage), then **Start**. **Start** generates the bracket first if you did not.

Once a stage has matches the button reads **Regenerate**. It leaves played matches alone: in a grouped stage only groups with no matches get a new bracket, an all-empty bracket simply gets its seeded teams written in, and anything else is blocked until you delete the existing matches.

**Back to draft** puts a ready stage back into draft, but only while none of its matches has started. **Complete** and **Reopen** move a live stage to finished and back; the platform also does this itself when the last match of a stage is closed or a result is corrected.

<Callout type="warning">
**Delete stage** removes the stage structure, its team slots and its generated matches. **Delete structure item** also removes that group's standings. Neither can be undone.
</Callout>

## Parallel stages

Each stage in the list shows its phase. Stages sharing a phase run in parallel — that is how a High and a Low division run side by side; a higher phase is a later wave, fed by the ones before it. Dragging a stage moves it between the existing phases; its **⋯** menu puts it **In parallel with** the stage listed above it, or **After** it (the stage and everything below it then move one phase later).

A stage wired to earlier stages in the flow cannot be marked ready until they are completed, so its seeds never freeze against standings that can still change.

## Seeding

The **Seeding** block of **Settings**.

- **Bracket seeds** decides who is seed 1 in the generated bracket: **Slot order (manual / standings)**, **Highest team avg SR first**, **Highest team total SR first** or **Random (stable per stage)**.
- **Seed by SR** (a menu item of the stage actions) spreads teams across the groups with a snake SR draft. Every manual assignment in the stage is cleared first.
- Who feeds the stage, and how many teams leave each group, are not set here but in the stage's **Who advances** block. Pick the source stage (**Takes participants from**) and a **Rule**: **Everyone**, **Top N overall**, **Top N from each group** or **By Swiss status**. For groups → playoff, choose **Top N from each group** on the playoff stage. The block is editable while the stage is in **Draft** and you can update the tournament; otherwise it is read-only and says why. A feed **Bracket** cannot express — a range, a manual pick, per-group overrides, merges, aggregates or a re-formation — is shown read-only with **Edit in Flow**. See [The Flow tab](/docs/organizers/flow).
- A stage fed by the team formation also shows **Teams formed by** in its **General** card: the method — **Balancer**, **Draft**, **Registered teams** or **Manual** — and, for **Draft**, the **Draft format**. They are saved with the stage by the same **Save changes** bar. The method can be changed while the formation is in **Draft**; the draft format is locked while a draft holds the roster.
- The stage seats its teams from its input when it turns **Ready**; the **Seeding** block's **Bracket seeds** decides the order they are placed in.

### Lower-bracket seeds in double elimination

In the playoff's **Who advances** block, the rule **Top N from each group** on a double-elimination stage adds **Start in the upper bracket, per group**: how many of each group's passed teams start in the upper bracket. Leave it empty and every passed team starts upper. The rest start in the lower bracket.

The playoff's **Lower bracket** item is created for you when the stages are wired — you do not add it by hand.

### The grand final

The **Grand final** field in **General**:

- **No reset** — the tournament ends at the grand final.
- **With reset** — if the lower-bracket team wins the grand final, an extra **Grand Final Reset** match is created. It appears only in that case: if the upper-bracket team wins, the grand final is the last match.

## Qualification and elimination

On a Swiss stage the **Qualification & elimination** card ends a team's Swiss early, the way a CS Major does with "three wins through, three losses out":

- **Auto-qualify after N wins** — a team with that many wins is qualified and is not paired again. A bye counts as a win.
- **Auto-eliminate after N losses** — a team with that many losses is eliminated and is not paired again. A draw counts as neither.

Each switch works on its own. Once fewer than two teams are left undecided, no further round is generated and the stage completes, even before **Swiss max rounds**. That field stays the cap: for every team to reach a verdict it has to be at least wins + losses − 1 (5 for 3 and 3), and the card warns when it is lower. A stage with a threshold is always played round by round, never generated as one round robin. Changing a threshold mid-stage takes effect from the next round.

Under a switched-on threshold, **Best-of of a qualification match** and **Best-of of an elimination match** set the series length of a match in which a team is one win from qualifying or one loss from elimination. It overrides the round's best-of; a match that is both plays the longer series. **Regular** keeps the usual rule.

Qualified and eliminated teams keep their places in the **Standings**, marked **Qualified** or **Eliminated**. Who goes on to the playoff is set in the playoff's **Who advances** block: **Top N overall**, **Top N from each group** or **By Swiss status**; for shapes the block cannot express see [The Flow tab](/docs/organizers/flow). With equal thresholds exactly half of an even group qualifies, so a **By Swiss status** rule set to **Qualified** passes that half.

## Points and tiebreakers

Points for a result are set on each stage, in **Bracket → Stages**: **Win points**, **Draw points**, **Loss points**. An empty field takes the default: 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw, 0 for a loss.

For Swiss, the same block has **Swiss bye points**: what a team scores for a round it sits out. It defaults to the win points.

**Standings preset** picks a ready-made set of tiebreakers (**System default (based on type)**, **Challonge Swiss (Buchholz first)**, **Challonge Round Robin**, **Default bracket**), and the **Tiebreaker evaluation order** list below lets you reorder the steps with the arrows or switch off the ones you do not want — a metric that is switched off is simply not evaluated.

The metrics are **Points**, **Head-to-Head**, **Median Buchholz**, **Buchholz**, **Match Wins** and **Score Differential**. **Points** is the ranking metric: it is always first and cannot be switched off.

**Reset to preset defaults** restores the order the chosen preset dictates.

### Pinned places

On **Bracket → Standings**, switch the view from **Table** to **Arrange**. Every standings table — a group, or a playoff stage — gets its own card. Drag a team to a place to pin it there: a pinned place holds no matter what results come later, and survives every recalculation. The other teams fill the remaining places in the order their results give them. Pinned rows carry a lock, in the admin and on the public table.

Teams that came out level on every tiebreaker are marked **Tied** — pinning one of them is how you decide the order. **Unpin** on a row releases that team; **Clear pins** releases the whole table.

Once a playoff seeded from a group has started, only the places below that group's qualifying cut can still change.

<Callout type="warning">
**Recalculate** rebuilds every standings row of the tournament from encounter results. Manual edits to individual rows are overwritten; pinned places are kept.
</Callout>

Standings count closed rounds only: while a round still has an unplayed encounter, it does not enter the table.

## Series length

For an elimination stage the series length is set on the **Bracket** tab, where the tree is: **Default best-of** above it, and the menu on every round header for that round — in double elimination the upper and lower bracket rounds are separate, and the deciding match is the final round (**Grand Final**). A change is saved at once and written into the matches that already exist; if some of them are played, reported or in progress, you are asked first, and scores are kept. The **Best-of** card of **Settings** is not shown for these stages.

Every other format (round robin, Swiss, individual Swiss, FFA) has one length for all matches: **All matches** in the **Best-of** card of **Settings** (for FFA, **Games per lobby**). It is baked into matches when they are generated; **Apply to existing matches** writes the saved value into matches that already exist, without regenerating. A single match gets its own length from its edit dialog.

## Custom bracket layout

The **Edit structure** mode of the **Bracket** tab (elimination stages only; **View** switches back) draws the bracket the stage will be generated from, without teams: each slot holds a seed placeholder (`U1` upper, `L1` lower) or the winner or loser of another match. Loser drops are the dashed connectors.

- Click a card's **W** or **L** output, then the slot it feeds. `Esc` cancels. A result can only feed one slot, so wiring it somewhere new empties the slot it left.
- A slot's own menu places a free seed or clears it; the card menu moves the card inside its round or deletes it, which also clears every slot that pointed at it.
- **+ match** adds a match to a column, **+ round** adds a column — on the upper section it goes in before the final.
- **Save** stays off while any slot is empty. Everything else is checked by the server, and what it refuses comes back as a list under the canvas; click a line to find the match it names.
- **U** and **L** are how many seeds the layout expects. When they differ from the seeds the stage really has, the stage refuses to generate until they match.
- **Reset to generated** deletes the custom layout and goes back to the one the format generates.

<Callout type="warning">
The layout can only be edited while the stage has no matches: once they exist, **Edit structure** is unavailable. Delete the generated matches first.
</Callout>

## How teams advance

- Only a completed match with a decided winner moves a team on.
- A draw cannot finish an elimination match. Try to record a level score and the platform refuses: **An elimination bracket needs a winner — a draw cannot be recorded here.**
- Change the winner of a match that has already been played and every result that depended on it is reset, so those matches have to be played again. See [Running matches](/docs/organizers/matches).
