The path from an empty account to your first match, in six steps. Each one has its own detailed article; this page is just the order.

## Six steps

1. **Sign in.** Press **Login** in the site header and pick a service: Discord, Battle.net or Twitch. There is no password here, and which services a given site offers is up to the community, so the sign-in screen only shows the available ones. See [Account and sign-in](/docs/players/account).
2. **Link your game accounts.** Open the account menu, then **Account settings** and the **Profile** tab. Battle.net is what puts your BattleTag and match statistics on your profile; Discord is what lets the bot send you invites and reminders. See [Account and sign-in](/docs/players/account).
3. **Find a tournament.** The **Tournaments** section in the site menu lists the community's tournaments and the stage each one is at. An open registration is shown on the tournament page itself. See [How a tournament works](/docs/players/tournaments).
4. **Register while registration is open.** Use **Register** on the tournament page: the form asks which roles you are willing to play, plus whatever the organizer added. Registration closes on the tournament's schedule, and a community may have entry requirements — a public Overwatch profile or an active subscription, for example. See [Registration](/docs/players/registration) and [Entry requirements](/docs/players/admission).
5. **Check in.** When check-in opens, a **Check-in** button appears on the participants page — it confirms that you are actually playing. The check-in window is short, and without it you will not be put on a roster. See [Check-in](/docs/players/check-in).
6. **Find your team and your match.** After check-in the rosters are built by the balancer or by a live draft, and your team appears on the tournament page. Then the matches are played: captains run the map and hero pick-ban and report the result, everyone else just plays. See [Draft](/docs/players/draft) and [Matches: pick-ban and results](/docs/players/matches).

<Callout>
If you bring your own team instead of joining the common pool, step 4 looks different: a captain invites players to the roster by link or directly. See [Captain teams and invites](/docs/players/captain-teams).
</Callout>

## So you do not miss anything

Check-in opening, a decision on your registration, a team invite and a scheduled match all land in the bell on the site. If your account has Discord linked, the bot repeats them as a direct message — and you can accept an invite or check in straight from a button in that message. See [Notifications and the Discord bot](/docs/players/notifications).
