Everything the site knows about your play lives on your profile: tournaments, matches, heroes, maps and achievements. This page covers what each section holds, where ranks and divisions come from, what MVP and impact actually mean, and which other statistics pages exist.

## Your profile

The header carries your name, avatar, divisions per role, recent form and the headline numbers: **Tournaments**, **Winrate**, **Maps**, **Avg Place**. Next to them sit **Copy link**, **Share**, **Player card** (downloadable or copyable as an image) and **Compare**.

Below that come the tabs:

| Tab | What is inside |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Career totals, placement spread, most played heroes, best teammates, recent encounters, an achievements preview and a breakdown of your last tournament |
| **Tournaments** | Your history event by event: the run through the bracket, map results and the team you played with |
| **Matches** | A match table with the **Wins**, **Losses**, **Draws**, **Group**, **Playoffs**, **Finals**, **MVP 1st** and **Has logs** filters, plus your most fought opponents |
| **Heroes** | Per hero: win rate, KDA, damage and the rest, compared against the global average, with maps and best games |
| **Maps** | Win rate by map and by game mode |
| **Achievements** | Badges earned and still locked — see [Achievements](/docs/players/achievements) |

If a player has never played a tournament, the tabs are replaced by a placeholder: there is nothing to count yet.

## Ranks and divisions

A rank is an SR value for one role. A **division** is a band of ranks on your community's grid — every community draws its own bands, so "Div 3" on one site and on another are not the same thing. The bands are occasionally redrawn; old ranks are carried over to the new layout so tournaments from different seasons stay comparable, and an individual tournament can stay pinned to the previous layout.

The rank you enter a tournament with is looked up in order, and the first source that holds a number wins:

1. The rank you typed into your application for that tournament.
2. The rank your community keeps for you.
3. Your game's own rank: for Overwatch the rank from your profile, if your BattleTag is linked; for League of Legends your Solo/Duo rank, if your Riot ID is linked; for Deadlock your rank, if your Steam account is linked. VALORANT has no such source: only the first two apply.

An empty field in the application does not mean "unranked" — it simply falls through to the next source.

**Rank history** is a chart built from snapshots of your game profile. Open it from a tournament's **Participants** list: expand a player's row (**Additional Details**). For Overwatch you can group the chart **By role** or **By account** and change the granularity — **Daily**, **Hourly** or **All points**. For League of Legends it shows Solo/Duo and Flex: tier and division, LP and wins/losses per queue, and a chart of both. For Deadlock it shows your current rank with its emblem and a chart of it. With no linked account there is no history: **Rank history is unavailable because this account is not linked to a registered site account.**

## MVP and impact

Both scores are computed from match logs. Until the organizer has uploaded the logs and they have been processed, a match carries neither — you can spot those matches by the empty **Logs** column.

- **MVP rating** shows how much a player stood out in that match. It appears as the **MVP** column in match and tournament tables, and on your profile as **MVP score** in the last-tournament breakdown.
- **Impact** answers a different question: did the player play better or worse than expected. The comparison is not against everybody, but against players of the same role and the same division, and the result is placed on a 0 to 100 scale.

A match where a player clearly went past their usual level is tagged **Above expectations**.

## Comparing players and heroes

**Compare** (menu **Players → Compare**) puts two players side by side. Pick a **Selected user**, then name an opponent under **Compare against** — or leave it empty and you are compared against the all-players average. The **Compare scope** switch chooses between **Overall performance** and **Hero / map performance**, and the role and division filters narrow the baseline to a comparable cohort. The built-in **How it works** guide walks through the steps.

**Characters leaderboard** (menu **Players → Characters leaderboard**) looks at it from the other side and answers **Who plays it best?** Pick a hero on the portrait rail, one row per class; a game with more than 60 characters (League of Legends) keeps a searchable picker instead. Below come the hero card (**Players ranked**, time on the hero, the median games per player under **Typical sample**, and **Best K/D** with its player), a **Top 10** chart and the table, where a cell's tint shows how many ranked players that result beats (50, 75 or 90%, with a legend). The **Rank by** chips re-order both the table and the chart, a click on a column re-ranks the table, and **Find a player** looks a player up by name while they keep their rank on the whole board.

Players with fewer games on the hero than **Min. games** are hidden by default: the floor is 5 games across all tournaments and 1 game when a single tournament is picked. The table's footer counts the hidden players, and **Show everyone** drops the floor to one game. The tournament filter (**All tournaments**) narrows the sample to one event. The page has no game tabs of its own: it shows the game picked in the site header's switcher, and under all games it takes the last game played and labels it with a "switch the game in the header" chip that opens that switcher.

## Matches and encounters

Two entries in the **Matches** menu collect games from across the platform:

- **Encounters** — head-to-heads between teams, split into live, upcoming and completed, with filters by tournament, format and closeness, search by team or player, and sorting by date or closeness. **Presets** narrow the list to finals, upsets, encounters with logs or your team's, and keep your own filter sets; **Summary** shows closeness, final scores, popular maps and the closest encounters under the same filters.
- **Matches** — individual maps that were played. A match page has the **Tables**, **Comparison** and **Timeline** tabs, the last of which replays the kill feed when logs have been parsed.

## Match statistics of Deadlock mixes

A Deadlock private lobby is not a public match: the game's data source, deadlock-api, publishes its statistics only once it knows the match's salts, the keys that unlock the replay. They reach it from players' computers through the deadlock-api-ingest program, so a mix match gets statistics when at least one player of the lobby runs it, when the host creates the lobby through the deadlock-api bot, or when the host pastes the match ID after the game.

To help your mix, install the program: the instructions are on [deadlock-api.com/ingest-cache](https://deadlock-api.com/ingest-cache), and for Windows there is a [ready installer](https://github.com/deadlock-api/deadlock-api-ingest/releases/latest/download/deadlock-api-ingest-setup.exe). Next to your seat in the mix room, the ingest badge shows that the program sent something in the last 14 days; without it the room tells you how to set it up. Finding a match by its players works when the Steam account you play on is linked to the site and your game profile is public: a private profile hides you from the search.

## Platform statistics

The **Statistics** page is the all-time **Tournament almanac**. Its header carries the totals: **Tournaments**, **Players**, **Encounters**, **Maps**, **Hours played** and **Tournament days**. Below them:

- **Players**: one table with the **Titles**, **Tournaments**, **Maps** (won and lost) and **Win rate** columns. Any column sorts it, titles by default; **Search players** looks a player up by name, and their rank does not change. A player with three tournaments or fewer has their win rate greyed out, and sorting by win rate keeps only those with more than three tournaments.
- **Records**: **Longest win streak**, **Biggest upset**, **Underdog champion** and **Closest final**. With one game picked, single-map records **From match logs** join them: hero damage, healing, eliminations, damage blocked, solo kills, most MVPs and the longest map. A record with no data is simply not on the page.

With one game picked, two more sections appear: **Characters** (each character's share of playtime, grouped by class; clicking a character opens the player leaderboard on it) and **Maps** (how many games each map saw, grouped by mode, with its share within the mode and its average length). At the bottom of the page, the **Encounter analytics** link leads to **Encounters**.

## Tournament analytics

The **Tournaments → Analytics** menu entry answers three questions: who surprised everyone, who is climbing or dropping, and how much those calls can be trusted. Pick a tournament and the page shows:

- **Predicted vs Actual** — where a team was expected to land and where it actually did. Teams that missed the forecast by four places or more show up under **Upsets**.
- A **Predicted move** for every player: **Climb**, **Drop** or **Hold** — in other words, whether they belong in a higher or a lower division next time.
- **Impact** and **Confidence** — the second one reflects how much played material sits behind the call. Few maps means low confidence.
- **Flags** — automatic hints raised when results look unusual.

<Callout>
Nothing on this page is a verdict. A flag is raised automatically for review, not as a ruling, and the division call is still the organizer's. The **How this works** button in the header walks through reading the page step by step, and tapping the ⓘ next to a figure shows what it means.
</Callout>

Analytics is run by the organizer, so a tournament that just finished may not have it yet.

## What other people see

Your public profile — name, avatar, tournament history and stats — is visible to anyone who can view your community. The one thing you configure separately is your stream: while the **Show my live stream on tournament pages** switch is off, your broadcast will not appear on tournament pages even if your application ticks the stream option. Your Twitch account still stays on your public profile. See [Account and sign-in](/docs/players/account).
