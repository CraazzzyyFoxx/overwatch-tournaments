A tournament travels from announcement to archive, and each step gives you something different to do. This page covers what every phase means, what the tournament page is made of, which stage formats exist and how the standings are read.

## Tournament phases

The current phase is shown as a badge on the tournament card and on the tournament page.

| Phase | What is happening | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Announced** | The tournament is public, applications are not open yet | Read the description, format and rules |
| **Registration** | Applications are being accepted | Submit your application, edit it or withdraw |
| **Check-in** | The organizer collects everyone who will actually play | Confirm that you are in |
| **Team formation** | Teams are being put together: by the balancer, or by captains picking players live | Wait for the rosters; in a draft, watch it and wait for your pick |
| **Live** | Teams play their matches | Play, run the pick-ban, report results |
| **Playoffs** | The elimination bracket is played | The same, except a loss now costs a lot |
| **Completed** | Every match is played | Look at the results, statistics and achievements |
| **Archived** | The tournament is out of the active list | The page stays readable |

Not every tournament goes through every phase: **Check-in**, for one, is optional. The organizer can skip a phase forward or move the tournament back to an earlier one — to reopen registration, for example.

The **Schedule** at the top of the **Overview** lists the phases with their times. The phase the tournament is in is highlighted, and when it has no time of its own it is marked **Now**. A phase whose window has closed is marked **Closed**. The next boundary — the start, or the close of registration — is counted down in the tournament header.

Two windows follow different rules, and the difference matters:

- **Registration** runs on its own schedule. It can be open or closed no matter which phase the tournament is currently in, and on a completed or archived tournament it is always closed. See [Registration](/docs/players/registration).
- **Check-in** only works while the tournament sits in the **Check-in** phase and only inside its own window. See [Check-in](/docs/players/check-in).

## Hidden tournaments

While an organizer is still preparing an event, they can keep it hidden. A hidden tournament does not appear in the tournament list, and its page answers that nothing was found — even through a direct link.

If the organizer gave you preview access, the page opens with the banner **Preview — hidden from the public site**. That means only workspace admins and invited preview users can see it, so sharing the link is pointless.

## The tournament page

Tournament sections sit in one rail of tabs. Some of them show up later than others.

| Section | What is inside | When it is available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The schedule; before the start the sign-ups by role, then the strongest teams and the first round's maps; during play the matches and the bracket; after it the podium; always how it's played (stages and their rules), the description and the organizer's links | Always |
| **Rules** | The tournament regulations with a table of contents | If the organizer published rules |
| **Participants** | Every application, by role or as a table, with search and a division filter | Always, though the organizer may hide the list itself and publish only the counts |
| **Maps** | The tournament map pool, per stage and per round | Always |
| **Teams** | Rosters with average SR and win-loss record | Once teams have been formed |
| **Bracket** | The stage bracket plus a switch to **Standings** | Once tournament matches have started |
| **Matches** | Every match with filters by stage, team and map | Once tournament matches have started |
| **Statistics** | The **Heroes** and **Maps** tabs for this tournament | Once tournament matches have started |
| **Streams** | Participant broadcasts | While the tournament runs and somebody is live |

The order of the tabs follows the phase: before play starts **Participants** sits near the front, and once matches begin it moves to the end to make room for the bracket and the matches. If a section is still locked, the hint explains why: **Available when tournament play begins.**, **The organizer has not published tournament stages yet.** or **Available once teams are formed.**

## How teams are built

The method is printed on the **Overview** under **How it's played**, next to the role slots of a team, and it decides what is expected from you after registration.

- **Balancer** — the site builds the teams. It spreads strong players evenly across rosters and tries to give everyone a role they asked for. All you provide is an application with your roles and ranks.
- **Draft** — captains pick players live, one at a time. See [Draft](/docs/players/draft).
- **Team registration** — you arrive as a ready roster led by a captain. See [Captain teams and invites](/docs/players/captain-teams).

## Stage formats

A tournament is made of stages. Usually that is a group stage followed by playoffs, but there can be more stages or fewer. Each format is printed on the **Overview** under **How it's played**, next to the stage name, with what the stage's rules say: the number of rounds, the length of a series, how a team goes through. Click the format to open a diagram that plays it out, with a short explanation.

- **Round-robin** — every team plays every other team. Placement comes from the standings.
- **Swiss** — a fixed number of rounds, and in each one opponents are matched by current score, so leaders meet leaders. The system avoids putting the same two teams together twice. With an odd number of teams somebody sits a round out, and the organizer sets how many points that is worth. The organizer may also end a team's Swiss early: after a set number of wins it is through, after a set number of losses it is out, and either way it plays no more rounds of the stage.
- **Single elimination** — one loss and the team is done.
- **Double elimination** — there is an upper and a lower bracket. The first loss drops a team into the lower bracket, the second knocks it out for good. Rounds are labelled **UB Round**, **UB Final**, **LB Round**, **LB Final** and **Grand Final**.
- **FFA** — teams are not paired off at all: the whole group plays in one **lobby**, several games in a row, and every game gives everyone a placement and a score at once.

<Callout>
In a double-elimination **Grand Final** the team from the upper bracket arrives with a cushion: it has never lost. If the lower-bracket team beats it, both sides are level on losses and a second match is created — the **Grand Final Reset**. It only appears in the bracket in that one case.
</Callout>

## Standings

On round-robin and Swiss stages the **Bracket** section has a switch over to **Standings**.

What the table holds:

- **A lock beside the place** — the organizer set that place by hand. It holds no matter what results come later.
- **W·D·L** — wins, draws and losses.
- **Pts** — the total under this tournament's scoring. How much a win, a draw and a loss are worth is the organizer's choice, and an individual stage may use different values.
- **H2H** — head-to-head. When teams are level on points, the first question is who beat whom directly.
- **Buchholz** — the strength-of-opposition tiebreaker: the tougher a team's schedule was, the higher the number. It is shown in two forms, median and full.
- **Form** — the recent run of results.
- **Adv** and **Out** — who moves on. On double elimination the table also marks the **Top N → upper bracket** cut.
- **Qualified** and **Eliminated** — on a Swiss stage with win or loss limits: the team has reached one and is not paired any more. It keeps its place in the table.

<Callout>
Standings count only fully played rounds. While a round still has an open match, it stays out of the maths — otherwise places would jump after every map.
</Callout>

Sometimes teams end up equal on every tiebreaker. The table says so plainly: **Equal on every tiebreaker. Order assigned, not earned.** The organizer may place such teams by hand.

## The lobby table

An FFA group has no matches you can open one by one: its result IS one lobby table. The same table opens from the lobby's own page and from the **Bracket** section.

What it holds:

- **Place** — the position in the group. Two teams sharing a place show the same number: no tiebreaker separated them. A lock beside it means the organizer set that place by hand.
- **Pts** — the total over every game played. A placement pays on the organizer's scale (first is worth more than second), and the score on top of it may pay too.
- **Games** — how many of the lobby's games this team already has counted.
- **Score** — whatever the organizer is counting: kills, points, anything. The column is headed with their own word, so it is named differently from tournament to tournament.
- **G1, G2, …** — one column per game of the lobby. The cell shows the placement on top and that game's score under it. **An empty cell means the game has not been entered yet** — it is not a zero.
- **Adv** and **Out** — who moves on; the **TOP N ADVANCE** line is drawn exactly where the advancing places end.

<Callout>
When the advance line runs through a tie, both teams are marked **Tie** rather than advancing: which of them goes through is decided by the assigned order, not by the games. The organizer may place such teams by hand.
</Callout>

The lobby code and the organizer's instructions arrive in the **lobby room** chat, linked from the lobby page. There is no map veto and no readiness check there: a lobby is played as one group, and the organizer enters each game's result.

## Streams

If participants stream on Twitch, their broadcasts are collected in the **Streams** section: the live channels, how many people are watching and how long each has been on air. While the tournament is played, the header of the **Overview** offers **Watch the broadcast**, and the organizer's official broadcast also docks in a corner of the page.

Your own stream is only surfaced there if you allowed it. The **Show my live stream on tournament pages** switch lives in your account settings and overrides everything else. See [Account and sign-in](/docs/players/account).
