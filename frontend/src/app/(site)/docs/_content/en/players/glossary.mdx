A short dictionary of the words you meet on the site, in plain language.

## Community and account

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Workspace | One community's own site: its own tournaments, ranks and achievements. Change it with **Switch workspace** in the header. |
| Player profile | Your in-game identity: BattleTag, match history, statistics, achievements. One account owns one player profile. |
| Unowned player profile | A player profile the community knows from match logs or an import, with nobody signed in behind it. It still appears in rosters and stats until its owner links it to their account. |
| BattleTag | Your Overwatch name in the "name#1234" form. |
| Smurf | An additional Overwatch account of the same player, listed separately on a registration. |
| Linked account | A Discord, Battle.net or Twitch account confirmed through OAuth. Used for sign-in, notifications and entry requirement checks. |

## Tournament

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Tournament | A competition inside one community, with its own registrations, stages and standings. |
| Phase | Where the tournament is in its lifecycle: announced, registration, check-in, draft, live, finished. The phase decides what you can do right now. |
| Schedule | The dates and times each phase opens and closes. |
| Stage | One competitive part of a tournament — a group stage or a playoff. Not the same as a phase: a phase is lifecycle, a stage is play. |
| Group stage | A stage where teams play inside groups and the best ones move on. |
| Round-robin | A format where every team plays every other team. |
| Swiss | A format with a fixed number of rounds where opponents are paired by current points. |
| Playoff | The knockout stage. |
| Bracket | The knockout tree: who plays whom, and where the winner goes next. Single or double elimination. |
| Standings | The results table of a stage: wins, losses, points, placement. |
| Seed | A team's ordering going into a stage. It shapes the bracket pairings and decides who bans a map first. |
| Tiebreakers | The rules that separate teams sitting on the same points. |
| Buchholz | A tiebreaker: the summed points of the opponents you faced. Tougher opponents mean a higher Buchholz. |
| Head-to-Head | A tiebreaker: who beat whom when they met. |
| Score differential | A tiebreaker: maps won minus maps lost. |

## Matches and maps

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Encounter | One pairing of two teams: the whole series of maps between them. |
| Match | One played map. Player statistics are counted per match. |
| Map pool | The set of maps a series draws from. |
| Candidate | A map or hero in the pool that can be banned or picked. |
| Pick-ban | The sequence in which the two sides take turns removing and choosing candidates: ban, pick, protect. It runs on maps and on heroes. |
| Slot | One map of a series. A slot can carry its own set of candidates. |
| Reserve map | The map the regulations hold back for a draw. |
| Report | The result a captain submits after play. Matching reports from both sides confirm the result; different ones dispute it. |
| Disputed | The two sides sent different results, so the organizer decides. |
| Match logs | The Overwatch log files an organizer uploads after play. All per-player statistics come from them. |

## Registrations and rosters

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Registration | Your application to a tournament: roles, top heroes, accounts and the organizer's questions. |
| Check-in | Confirming, inside a set window, that you will actually play. |
| On call | A mark on your registration: you play as usual, and you are fine being called in if somebody drops. |
| Admission | The organizer's entry checks before a tournament: a public Overwatch career profile and a subscription. |
| Roster | A team's line-up: who plays on which slot. |
| Roster slot | One place in a line-up, for a specific role or for flex. |
| Substitute | A player beyond the starting line-up who comes in for somebody else. |
| Captain | The player who builds the team, sends invites, and is responsible for pick-ban and reporting the result. |
| Invite | An offer to take a team slot, either addressed to an account or as a link with a limited lifetime. |
| Balancer | Automatic team building: it spreads strong players across teams and tries to give everyone a role they want. |
| Draft | The live alternative to the balancer: captains take turns picking players, with a clock on every pick. |
| Roles | Tank, Damage, Support. Flex means you are happy on any of them. |

## Scrims and mixes

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Scrim | A practice encounter outside any tournament: a room with map veto, hero bans and per-map results. The opponent joins by link. |
| Mix | A community custom game: the host gathers a pool of players, splits it into two teams and records the result. |
| Bench | The mix players sitting this game out. Rotation keeps the sitting fair across games. |

## Ranks and statistics

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Rank | The number describing a player's strength on a role. In a tournament the site takes the rank on your registration first, then the community's rank, then your Overwatch rank. |
| SR | The numeric value of an Overwatch rank. |
| Division | A band of ranks — the shelf a player is placed on. |
| MVP rating | How much a player contributed to a match compared with others on the same role. MVP achievements are based on it. |
| Playtime | The share of a match a player spent on a given hero. |
| Achievement | A badge for meeting a condition. Some are computed from match data, others are granted by the organizer by hand. |
| Rank history | The chart of your Overwatch rank over time, built from a linked BattleTag. |
