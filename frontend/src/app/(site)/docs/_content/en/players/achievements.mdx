Achievements are badges for what you did in tournaments: titles, hero mileage, win streaks, the route you took through the bracket. This page covers where to find them, how they are put together, and why the same badge showed up for you and not for the player next to you.

## The achievements catalog

The **Players → Achievements** menu entry is the full list of what can be earned in your community. A rarity strip sits on top, then the filter bar, then the badges themselves.

- The rarity strip and the filter chips narrow the list to one tier: **Mythic**, **Legendary**, **Epic**, **Rare**, **Uncommon**, **Common**.
- Next to them are the category chips.
- The order of the list comes from the sort picker: **Sort: Rarity**, **Sort: Name**, **Sort: Earned**.
- Search matches badge names.
- The summary on top: **Total Achievements**, **Rarest**, **Total Earned**.

Opening a badge shows its **Conditions** — the exact set of requirements that has to line up before it is awarded. Conditions can be joined with **AND**, **OR** and **NOT**: "won the tournament" **AND** "had never played before", for instance. **See who earned it** opens the badge's own page with the **Share of players**, the **Times awarded** count and the list of everyone holding it. If nobody has it yet, the page says so: **No one has earned this achievement yet.**

## Rarity and categories

Rarity is not assigned by hand — it is the share of players who hold the badge.

| Rarity | Share of players |
| --- | --- |
| **Mythic** | under 1% |
| **Legendary** | 1–5% |
| **Epic** | 5–15% |
| **Rare** | 15–30% |
| **Uncommon** | 30–50% |
| **Common** | over 50% |

The category says what the badge is about: **Overall**, **Hero**, **Division**, **Team**, **Standing**, **Match**. A separate pill shows its scope: a **Global** badge is measured across your whole history, **Tournament** ties it to one event, **Match** to a single match.

## How achievements are awarded

There are two routes, and they add up.

1. **Automatically.** The organizer uploads match logs, the logs are processed, and the achievement conditions are then checked against the fresh data. Everything that matches is awarded on its own.
2. **By hand.** An organizer can grant a badge directly — for something the automatic rules cannot see — or take an awarded one back. A manual decision outranks the automatic result.

<Callout>
Until a match's logs are uploaded and processed, no achievements come out of that match. The same goes for MVP and impact — see [Profile, stats and ranks](/docs/players/stats). If the logs are missing, ask your organizer to upload them.
</Callout>

When an organizer changes a badge's conditions, results are recalculated under the new rules — a badge can appear retroactively for a tournament you played long ago, or disappear.

## When they show up

Badges do not land at the moment of victory; they land after the logs are processed, which usually means during the tournament or shortly after it. A manual grant is visible as soon as the organizer applies it. There is no "you earned an achievement" notification — check the **Achievements** tab on your profile.

## Where they appear on your profile

- The **Achievements** tab — the full list, each badge marked **Unlocked** or **Locked**, with a "how many of how many" counter. The same bar carries the rarity filters, search, sorting (**Sort: Rarity**, **Sort: Name**, **Sort: Earned**) and a tournament picker — **All tournaments** or **Without tournament**.
- The **Achievements** block on the **Overview** tab — a short showcase of your standout badges.
- A badge card inside your profile shows where it came from: an **Earned in** line with the tournament or match, and a **· earned ×N** note when it was awarded more than once. For older records it may say that the tournament or match details were not kept.
- If you do not hold the badge, the card says so: **You haven't earned this achievement yet.**

For a player with nothing yet, the tab reads: **This user has no achievements yet.**
