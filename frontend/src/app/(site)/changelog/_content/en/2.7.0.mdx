<Lead>Mix matches get statistics: from an Overwatch log, or from a Deadlock match the site finds on its own. A Deadlock match has its own view with builds, lanes and fights, players are named the way that game knows them, and hero bans and picks in mixes are easier to run.</Lead>

### Mix statistics

The host uploads an Overwatch log from the lobby room (automatic upload from the log folder works too) or from a match row in the history. It gives the same player statistics with MVP places as a tournament map. They show only inside the mix: mix matches don't count toward profiles or records.

The site finds a Deadlock match on its own: once the result is recorded, it looks for the private lobby the round's teams played in and pulls in the statistics. Players need a linked Steam account for this. If the match isn't found, the host enters its ID with **Import statistics by match ID** on the history row.

### Match statistics

The match statistics window is rebuilt: a header with the map and score, a switcher across the series' maps and three tabs — scoreboard, breakdown and a timeline grouped by fights. Each can be read for the whole map or a single round.

A Deadlock match has its own view. A strip above the tabs shows the whole match: the souls lead, kills, fights, destroyed structures and mid bosses. Drag along it and every tab shows the match at that moment.

- **Teams** — both teams side by side, like the in-game scoreboard, with each player's build.
- **Table** — Overview, Combat, Economy and Support column sets. A row opens the player panel: the build path minute by minute, damage to each enemy and by source.
- **Breakdown** — damage, healing and damage taken per team, the match's turning points and every player's souls over time.
- **Lanes** — who won each lane by 9:00 and how its lead moved over the match.
- **Timeline** — every fight and pick in order, with the structures that fell during it.

Sides wear the game's colours. The view opens wherever a match has statistics: on tournament maps, in a mix's history and on the match page.

### Names per game

On one game's pages — rosters, registrations, pick-ban, matches, statistics, leaderboards, drafts and mixes — a player is named the way that game knows them. First comes the handle of a verified game account (Battle.net, Riot or Steam), then the community name, then the handle of an unverified account. Steam shows the profile name, not the number. Hovering shows the main name.

Pages about every game — the profile, achievements and search — keep the main name. With a game selected, the profile adds the handle in that game under the name. Search also finds a player by their public Steam name.

### Hero bans and picks in mixes

In Deadlock heroes are picked in turn or in two phases, and the host sets the ban and pick clocks. The pick order can be freed: a team's turn then goes to any player who still has no hero, or every pick is left to the captains. Tapping a player's row chooses whom a pick is for.

The ban room says whose turn it is above the pool, shows the order of turns as a track of slots in the teams' colours, and finds a hero by name.

A game with a single competitive map always plays it: the lobby card, the room, **Your seat** and the Discord lineup card name that map.
